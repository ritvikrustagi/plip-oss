Case 01 — Algebra I

The class that stopped
copying answers.

Public high school · Algebra I, period 2 · 31 students · 9 weeks

1,900

hints logged over nine weeks

15 min

of class time back, most days

6

students flagged before the unit test, not after

74%

of hints marked helpful by the student

  • The problem Homework came back finished and correct, and told her nothing. She spent the first fifteen minutes of most lessons re-teaching to the room because she couldn’t tell who needed it.
  • What we did She uploaded her syllabus and her lesson notes for the solving-equations unit. Students installed the Chrome extension and kept using the same online practice set they already had.
  • What Jade did Held back answers. On 3x + 5 = 20 it asked what would undo the + 5 and waited. Every hint, and whether the student said it helped, landed on her dashboard the same minute.
  • What changed She stopped re-teaching to the room. Tuesday mornings she arrived with a list of six names and sat with them while the rest moved on.
  • What didn’t work Four students found the hint-only approach annoying in week one and went back to a chatbot. Two came back when Jade started showing a worked example on a different problem.
Case 02 — Chemistry

Finding the gap a test
would have missed.

Independent school · Chemistry, two sections · 24 students · 6 weeks

11

students asking the same question

1

reteach that moved the whole unit

820

hints logged

2 days

from signal to lesson plan

  • The problem Stoichiometry homework scored normally. The teacher suspected something underneath was wrong but had no way to see it.
  • What Jade did Grouped the hints it was giving by topic. Eleven of twenty-four students were stuck on unit conversion — a prerequisite from the term before, not the unit being taught.
  • What changed One forty-minute reteach on dimensional analysis, two days after the signal appeared. The next problem set was the first all term without a long tail of failures.
  • What didn’t work Jade read handwritten lab work poorly. Those sessions were the least useful and we excluded them from the write-up rather than pad the numbers.
